Theses & Research Projects

Our group offers a range of topics for Bachelor’s and Master’s theses — the latter both in Computer Science and in Scientific Computing — as well as introductory and advanced research and software projects. All topics are closely connected to our ongoing research and projects.

Please read this whole page carefully before getting in touch. It describes what we expect and how the process works, from first contact to the final presentation. It is also worth looking through our completed theses to get a sense of the topics we supervise. We advise many theses each semester and, unfortunately, cannot accommodate every request.

Overview

We offer thesis topics for both Bachelor and Master students, connected to our active research in natural language processing, information retrieval, knowledge graphs, question answering, and conversational AI — often with an interdisciplinary link to medicine, mental health, law, politics, or education. Master’s theses are possible both in Computer Science and in Scientific Computing.

Before applying, please review the requirements below, look at the topics of past theses, and make sure your interests genuinely align with the group’s research.

Requirements

Coursework

You should have completed advanced courses offered by our group, such as:

  • Data Science for Text Analytics (IDSTA)
  • Complex Network Analysis (ICNA)
  • Natural Language Processing with Transformers (INLPT)

You should also have some background in machine learning, ideally in combination with NLP. We strongly recommend that — especially before a Bachelor’s thesis — you first complete an advanced software practical to become familiar with the data and tools we use across our projects.

Programming and technical skills (required)

  • Very good programming experience with Python (strongly preferred), including libraries such as pandas and numpy
  • A solid background in statistics and linear algebra
  • Familiarity with tools such as Git and Docker

Helpful, but not required

  • Machine-learning frameworks such as PyTorch
  • NLP frameworks such as spaCy, gensim, or LangChain
  • Experience with OpenSearch or Elasticsearch
  • Graduate courses in efficient algorithms (e.g. IEA1) and machine learning (e.g. IML, IFML, IAI), and frameworks such as scikit-learn or Keras

A note on the minimum background

If you have only taken the undergraduate course Introduction to Databases (IDB) and none of the courses above, it is unlikely that we can accommodate your request. Please also familiarize yourself with the examination regulations (Prüfungsordnung) of your specific study program.

Getting in Contact

Once you have read this page and are confident that your interests align with our research, send an email to Prof. Michael Gertz with the subject line “Anfrage BSc Arbeit” or “Anfrage MSc Arbeit”. Please include:

  • your current transcript as a PDF (downloadable from the LSF),
  • information about your field of application (Anwendungsfach) and the relevant courses you have completed,
  • your programming experience and the projects you have worked on,
  • the areas of our research you are most interested in, and
  • anything else that helps us understand your request.

We will review your materials and, if there is a good fit, arrange an in-person meeting to discuss possible topics and next steps.

Thesis Exposé

Once a topic has been agreed upon, and before official registration, you write a short exposé (about 4–6 pages). It demonstrates your approach to scientific work and writing, and typically becomes the basis for the introductory chapter of your thesis — so the effort is not wasted. The exposé should cover:

  • the context of the project and the surrounding research,
  • the problem statement(s),
  • your objectives and planned approach,
  • related work, and
  • milestones for completing the thesis on time.

Getting a good overview of the related work early on is particularly important; your advisor will give you some starting points. You submit the exposé to your advisor on an agreed schedule; occasionally, students present their research plan in a short (10–15 minute) talk to the group for feedback.

Official Registration

After a satisfactory exposé, you and your advisor agree on an official start date and you complete the registration form for your program:

  • Bachelor’s thesis — form from the Computer Science department,
  • Master’s thesis in Computer Science — form from the Computer Science department,
  • Master’s thesis in Scientific Computing — form obtained from Mrs. Kiesel.

Hand the completed form to Prof. Michael Gertz, who signs it and submits it to the relevant examination office.

Research & Advising

Your thesis is ultimately your own work on the agreed topic. After registration, you schedule the next steps with your advisor. Please do not expect us to inquire about your progress — if we have to ask, it is usually a sign that you are falling behind schedule.

  • Meetings. Bachelor’s and Master’s theses typically involve bi-weekly meetings (or meetings on demand). They serve to answer questions and discuss how to proceed based on your findings and ideas.
  • Preparation. Send the topics you want to discuss — for example thesis sections you have written — to your advisor ahead of each meeting.
  • LaTeX. All theses must be written in LaTeX, the scientific standard in computer science, using our thesis template (see below).
  • Language. We prefer and encourage English, the language of research and most of IT and computer science; German is permitted.
  • Scientific writing. You are responsible for ensuring your thesis meets the standards of a scientific paper. Matters of format and style are covered in the seminars that are part of your curriculum.
  • Academic integrity. Follow the university’s rules on good academic practice and against plagiarism.

You may also request copies of past theses from members of the group for orientation.

Thesis Template

You must use our LaTeX thesis template. It is designed for a Master’s thesis in Computer Science written in German, but it can be adapted for a Bachelor’s thesis or for a thesis written in English. You may improve the template within reason.

We provide the template (as a .zip archive) together with a sample PDF, as well as detailed guidance on writing seminar papers with the template, including presentation slides in English and German. (Download links will be added here.)

Final Presentation

After submitting your thesis to the examination office (Mrs. Sopka for Computer Science, Mrs. Kiesel for Scientific Computing), you coordinate with your advisor to schedule your presentation. For Master’s theses, the second reviewer’s availability must also be taken into account. We arrange a room once the date and time are fixed.

  • Public. The presentation is open to the public and announced via the department’s mailing lists.
  • Length. You have roughly 20–25 minutes. Keep in mind that part of the audience is not familiar with your research, so clearly state the problem setting and objectives.
  • Style. Presentations should meet scientific standards — avoid fancy animations or distracting color schemes, and include slide numbers to support the discussion afterwards.

We provide LaTeX Beamer presentation templates in English and German, with sample PDFs. (Download links will be added here.)
